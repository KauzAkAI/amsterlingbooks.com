Fed & macro drivers Policy

Fed decisions, inflation prints, jobs reports — the underlying forces.

CPI · May 12

April CPI rose 3.8% YoY — hottest since May 2023

Headline +0.6% m/m on energy shock; core +0.4% m/m, +2.8% YoY. Shelter re-accelerated +0.6%. Gasoline +28.4% YoY.

→ Pushed 10-Yr Treasury near a 1-year high; rate-cut odds collapsed.

FOMC · Apr 29

Fed holds at 3.50–3.75% — four dissents, most since 1992

Sharp split on the committee. Next meeting June 16–17 includes updated dot plot. Markets price ~30% odds of a HIKE in 2026.

→ Removes cushion that had been keeping mortgage rates lower.

Jobs · May 8

April payrolls +115K, unemployment steady at 4.3%

Cooler than March's +185K but beat consensus +55K. Health care +37K, transport +30K, retail +22K. Participation fell to 61.8%.

→ Mixed signal; not weak enough to force a Fed cut.

Geopolitics · ongoing

Iran conflict pushing oil higher, lifting inflation expectations

Energy shock that began in February has stalled disinflation and is feeding into shipping & goods costs.

→ Single biggest reason mortgage rates are ~50 bps higher than January.

Housing market Sector

Home sales, inventory, builder & mortgage application data.

MBA · May 7

Mortgage applications fall 4.4% week-over-week

Purchase apps -3% w/w but +5% YoY — affordability still gating activity. Refi softened with the rate backup.

→ Lender demand metric; thinner pipelines = competitive rates for qualified borrowers.

Inventory · May

Inventory +2.3% YoY; 1.47M unsold homes end of April

Up 5.8% from March, up 1.4% YoY. Median list price -2.2% YoY. Roughly 36% of listings have had a price cut.

→ Shifting toward buyers, but high rates still suppressing turnover.

Pending sales · May 7

Pending home sales jump 10% YoY

4-week rolling average 6.5% ahead of 2025. Homes take 43 days to go under contract (40 last year); new listings -1.8% YoY.

→ Demand more resilient than headlines suggest, despite the 6%+ rate environment.

Prices · April

Equity-rich homeowner share fell — but home prices nudged up

HELOC balances cooling as the % of "equity rich" homes (loan ≤50% of value) declined; prices still rising modestly nationally.

→ HELOC volume sensitive to both rates and tappable equity.

How to read this: Mortgage rates move primarily with the 10-Year Treasury, which itself responds to inflation, Fed expectations, and risk sentiment. The Fed Funds rate sets short-term rates and is the anchor for Prime, SOFR, HELOC, and most variable consumer loans. Today's bigger picture: an Iran-driven energy shock revived inflation, the Fed has paused with internal disagreement, and markets now price a possible HIKE rather than a cut — keeping mortgage rates near 6.5%.
